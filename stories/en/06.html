RecordWeb · Trust perspective

Trust is verifiable.

How records can be linked across organisational and system boundaries, without requiring blind trust.

Not "trust us."
But "check the rules this record was created under."
This presentation complements the stories about everyday work, architecture, and handover. At its centre is the trust question: why may one organisation reference another's record instead of copying it? And why can an archive entrust records to a responsible application as long as it's in operation? The answer isn't a mere assurance, but a verifiable framework.

Scene 1 · Linking instead of replicating

A network of records requires trust between organisations.

Parliament

Runs the case for the parliamentary question.

Question
own record, own context

Trusts that the government won't change its answer after finalisation.

↔

Government

Runs the case for answering it.

Answer
own record, own context

Trusts that parliament won't change its question afterwards.

The alternative would be replication: each side copies the other's records. RecordWeb instead relies on self-contained records and explicit relationships.
The relationship between question and answer doesn't have to be a data copy. Each organisation stays responsible for its own records. That's exactly why the trust question arises in both directions: does the linked record stay reachable? Does a finalized version stay unchanged? Is it later traceable under which rules it was created?

Scene 2 · Trust must not be blind

An assurance alone is not proof.

?
Parliament to government

Why shouldn't the answer be changed after finalisation?

?
Government to parliament

Why should the question stay durably resolvable, if we only link to it?

?
Archive to application

Why should the application maintain records reliably for years, only handing them over at EoL?

Self-custody doesn't mean "the application can do whatever it wants." It means responsibility is delegated to an application only within a clear, verifiable framework.
The self-custody consideration must stay precise here: an application doesn't automatically become an archive system. The right question is: under which conditions can a responsible application maintain records itself over a longer period? The answer needs rules, evidence, and, if applicable, independent review.

Scene 3 · What must be verifiable

Responsibility for records translates into concrete verification areas.

1Identity

Does every record get a stable, resolvable reference point?

2Integrity

Is the version clearly recognisable and protected after finalisation?

3Relationships

Do relationships to the case and further records stay traceable?

4Availability

Is it clear who provides the record and where it's currently resolved?

5Transition

Is a controlled custodyTransfer planned for an end of life?

These five areas don't form a complete standard, but an understandable trust map. Detailed test cases, profiles, and test suites can emerge from them for concrete conformance checking. In the Question Time PoC, for example, DID resolution, schema binding, payload validation, snapshot integrity, and immutable finalisation are implemented.

Scene 4 · From rulebook to evidence

Rules are described. Applications are assessed. The result stays traceable.

SchemaRecord

Defines the rules for a RecordType: structure, required fields, allowed states, and transitions.

→
Conformance assessment

A responsible or independent assessment body evaluates a concrete application and product version.

→
ConformanceRecord

Records the claim, subject, evidence, method, results, and validity period as its own record.

Not a PDF certificate next to the system, but a self-identifiable, versionable, and verifiable proof of conformance.
Depending on governance, the assessment body can be the archive, a responsible public oversight body, or an independent, internationally recognised assessment organisation. What matters is the role: the assessment body doesn't attest abstract "good quality," but assesses a concrete subject against defined requirements.

Scene 5 · The SchemaRecord in the PoC

The schema describes how a parliamentary answer is created and finalized.

In the Question Time PoC, the SchemaRecord for the RecordType "Question Time – Answer" is likewise finalized and versioned. Among other things, it defines required fields and the transition from draft to finalized.

{
  "did": "did:rwp:b7d4c810:schema:fragestunde-antwort",
  "type": "SchemaRecord",
  "payload": {
    "displayName": "Question Time – Answer",
    "allowedStates": ["draft", "finalized"],
    "stateTransitions": [
      { "from": "draft", "to": "finalized", "requiresOwnerSignature": true }
    ],
    "required": ["antworttext", "bundesratdid", "beantwortetam"],
    "conformanceRecords": []
  },
  "metadata": { "state": "finalized", "schemaVersion": "sha256:b03e4ff…" }
}
This slide shows an actual, cleaned excerpt of the SchemaRecord from the Question Time PoC, not a hypothetical model. Two things matter: first, the schema describes the finalisation-relevant transition and requires the owner's signature. Second, the SchemaRecord contains references to conformance records, so the conformance relationship isn't only claimed organisationally but anchored in the rule artefact itself.

Conformance refers to a concrete application and product version.

Scene 8 · Mutual verifiability

Conformance doesn't create a trust advance. It enables control in both directions.

✓

The application's responsible party

Can check whether the conformance reference embedded in the schema used is valid and applicable.

  • Does the ConformanceRecord DID match?
  • Does the bound snapshot match?
  • Is the evidence still valid?
  • Does it refer to the current product version?
✓

Attesting body, e.g. an archive

Can check whether its conformance evidence is correctly referenced and unchanged within the SchemaRecord.

  • Is its own ConformanceRecord used?
  • Does its SnapshotHash match?
  • Which application and version were assessed?
  • Which tests and evidence exist?
Trust isn't issued once and then forgotten. It stays verifiable as a relationship between rules, assessment, and application.
This slide addresses bidirectional control. The organisation responsible for the application can check the evidence in the schema. The attesting body can, conversely, check whether its statement is correctly and unaltered embedded. Because the ConformanceRecord has a validity period, it's also clear: conformance is bound to a product state and time, not an unlimited quality claim.

Scene 9 · Trust until the transition

When no successor application takes over, AIS steps in.

The conformance framework strengthens trust in the responsible application as long as it's operated. At end of life, a controlled custodyTransfer is required.

RWP-conformant application

Manages and provides the finalized record under defined and verifiable rules.

→
End of Life

No successor application takes over the record and its provisioning.

→
AIS

Takes over the existing record within a controlled handover framework.

Conformance doesn't replace the transition. It makes it more credible that the application can reliably manage the record until that transition.
This slide connects the conformance story with the archive story. Finalisation doesn't trigger archiving. As long as an RWP-conformant application is operated, the record stays there. If no successor application is available and the application reaches EoL, AIS is the defined path. The record stays the same; a SIP can form the handover framework.

Scene 10 · Steering through verifiable requirements

Conformance becomes an instrument for governance and architecture.

For archives

Requirements for custody and later transferability of records can start earlier, at rules and the environment of creation.

For organisations

They can link records instead of replicating them, when responsibility and conformance are traceable.

For procurement and architecture

Conformance profiles can gradually become verifiable requirements for new or renewed applications.

Perspective: An abstract duty to keep orderly records becomes a concrete, testable, and documented property of applications.

The discussion around self-custody can build on this. RecordWeb doesn't pre-empt it, but offers a basis for delegating responsibility not blindly, but based on verifiable conformance.
The governance effect is the real strategic benefit. Conformance makes it possible to bring requirements early into architecture, procurement, and further development. It doesn't replace legal or organisational responsibility, but it makes technical and procedural commitments verifiable.
