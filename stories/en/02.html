RecordWeb · Architecture perspective

Network, not islands.

From application-bound records management to a networked information architecture.

Not another central system. A network of self-contained records.
Welcome. This story looks at RecordWeb from an architecture standpoint: how connected records can span applications and organisations without becoming a single, centralised system.

Scene 3 · Where context is lost today

The system of record often sees only part of the actual work.

Governance model

  • The case is managed in the system of record
  • Relevant documents are transferred cleanly
  • Metadata and assignment are complete
  • The dossier reflects the business context

Actual working landscape

  • Draft answers emerge in office tools
  • Coordination happens in chat and by email
  • Subject-matter knowledge sits in specialised applications
  • Transfers and assignments happen afterwards, if at all
The governance model describes an orderly world. The actual working landscape looks different. This gap is where relevant context gets lost today.

Scene 4 · The architecture shift

From the container as centre to the record as primary object.

Today: context through containers

RecordWeb: context through relationships

Today, context is mostly created by placing documents into the same container — a folder, a dossier. RecordWeb shifts context-creation to explicit relationships between self-contained records.

Scene 5 · The shared architecture layer

RecordWeb gives distributed applications a common language.

Identity

A record receives a durable identifier that can be referenced across applications.

Relationships

Question, processing, answer, and further records are explicitly linked to one another.

Resolution

A resolver mediates where a record can currently be reached, within its responsibility and hosting context.

Identity, relationships, and resolution form the shared vocabulary that lets distributed applications speak about the same records without owning each other's data.

Scene 7 · Three states of the same landscape

The architecture can grow application by application.

The transition architecture accepts that applications have different levels of maturity.

Today

Business applications, Teams, and other tools generate information. Adoption into records management happens partly after the fact and manually.

Transition

The business application is RWP-capable. MiniChat and Teams are connected via SoX connectors.

Target picture grows

A newly procured MiniChat is RWP-native. Teams initially stays integrated via SoX, until a natural renewal point is reached there too.

Important: Not all systems need to reach the same level of maturity at the same time. The architecture supports the coexistence of native RWP capability and integration.

Scene 8 · The normal development framework

RecordWeb isn't rolled out. It's built in with every change.

1

Orient

Identify relevant information flows and the applications where context can be lost today.

2

Integrate

Connect suitable first cases to RecordWeb via a layer such as SoX.

3

Renew

Take RWP capability into account architecturally during further development, replacement, and procurement.

4

Expand

The network gradually extends across further applications, organisations, and business processes.

Procurement as a lever: Public authorities renew applications through regulated procedures anyway. RecordWeb capability can initially be considered a quality aspect and later become a binding part of tender requirements.

RecordWeb grows through the normal cycle of orienting, integrating, renewing, and expanding — not through a rollout project.

Scene 9 · The architecture decision

The question isn't: "Which system replaces which?"

The strategic question is: which shared architecture layer do we need so that records from different applications stay identifiable, linkable, and reachable across their entire lifespan?

Without a shared layer

  • New applications create new information boundaries
  • Context is copied, exported, or transferred manually
  • Integration stays ad hoc and project-based
  • Long-term traceability is reconstructed after the fact

With RecordWeb as the target framework

  • New applications become interoperable step by step
  • Records stay networked through relationships
  • Integration and native capability can coexist
  • Traceability becomes part of the originating architecture
The real question is architectural, not about replacing one system with another.

Scene 10 · What belongs on the roadmap

A first architectural step is small, but sets a direction.

1. Confirm the target picture

Recognise RecordWeb as the long-term reference framework for networked, cross-application records.

2. Use the pilot architecture

Use the Question Time PoC — with question management, answer management, MiniChat, Teams integration, SoX, conformance checking, MiniChat migration through to archiving — as a learning environment.

3. Align renewals

Deliberately check interoperability during suitable further developments, replacements, and procurements.

A first step doesn't need to be large. It needs to point in the right direction and use the pilot architecture as a learning environment.
