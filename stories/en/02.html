RecordWeb · Architecture Perspective

Network instead of islands.

From application-bound records management to a networked information architecture.

No big-bang migration. A long-term architecture decision.
This presentation is aimed at architects. It answers a strategic question: How can an organization gradually develop its heterogeneous application landscape into a networked, long-term traceable information architecture?

Scene 1 · The target vision

Records can be connected across organizational and application boundaries.

In the Question Time PoC, Parliament and Government each run their own case. Individual records are nevertheless linked to one another, without replicating them or copying them into a shared dossier.

Two organizations, two cases, each with several applications, but no central file container. What matters is: The question record and the answer records can explicitly relate to and be linked within the counterparty's business. Each side retains its own responsibility and manages its own records. Networking does not replace replication through centralization, but through reliable relationships.

Scene 2 · Today's integration spider web

Today, a new information island grows with every application.

Business applications, collaboration tools, office files, email, and records management systems each form their own data and context boundaries.

The core problem is not a missing system. What is missing is a shared, cross-application layer for identity, relationships, responsibility, and long-term resolution of records.

Do not talk about individual technical weaknesses of products here. The architectural critique is structural: Every application models its objects and contexts for its own purpose. The central records management system is meant to later capture the relevant traces. This creates interfaces, handover points, replicas, and manual after-the-fact documentation – and the spider web keeps growing with every new application.

Scene 3 · Where context gets lost today

The System of Record often sees only part of the actual work.

Governance model

  • The business is conducted in the System of Record
  • Relevant documents are transferred cleanly
  • Metadata and assignment are complete
  • The dossier reflects the business context

Actual work landscape

  • Draft answers are created in office tools
  • Coordination happens via chat and email
  • Domain knowledge resides in specialized applications
  • Transfers and assignments happen afterward, if at all
Not because employees don't consider traceability important. But because the actual task is already completed by the time the information would still need to be transferred afterward.
A brief reference to the first presentation about Ms. Roth is enough. For architecture guidance, what matters is: Manual capture processes are a risk in the target architecture because they depend on behavior under time pressure. The architectural question is therefore: How can the relevant context arise where the work actually takes place?

A case or dossier remains important as a view. But it no longer has to be the only place where relationships and meaning are established. It becomes a view onto a connected context made up of records.
The point is not: abolish dossiers. They remain central for business steering, orientation, and user guidance. The shift is: A dossier, or case, becomes a view formed from records and relationships. This allows a record to appear in several legitimate contexts without being copied or filed multiple times.

Scene 5 · The shared architecture layer

RecordWeb gives distributed applications a common language.

Identity

A record receives a persistent identifier that can be referenced across applications.

Relationships

Question, processing, answer, and other records are explicitly linked to one another.

Resolution

A resolver mediates where a record can be reached within the current context of responsibility and provisioning.

RWP does not create an additional monolithic data container, but a protocol for a network of records.
For architecture guidance, the central idea is: Interoperability lies not only in point-to-point interfaces, but in a shared layer for distributed records and their context.

Scene 6 · Integration instead of replacement

Today's application landscape does not need to disappear.

Where an application does not yet natively support RWP, an integration layer can establish the connection to RecordWeb.

Existing applicationChat, business application, or collaboration tool remains the place of work.
→
SoXIntegration and connector layermaps relevant work to the RecordWeb context
→
RecordWebRecords, relationships, identities, and resolvable endpoints.
Integration is not a workaround alongside the target architecture. It is the path into the target architecture.
In the PoC, SoX stands for this bridging function. It does not replace the source application. Nor is it another dossier into which people copy content. It mediates between existing applications and the RWP environment in the sense of a system integration. This makes gradual modernization possible without having to wait for a complete replacement.

Scene 7 · Three states of the same landscape

The architecture can grow application by application.

The transitional architecture accepts that applications have different levels of maturity.

TodayBusiness applications, Teams, and other tools generate information. Adoption into records management happens partly after the fact and manually.
→
TransitionBusiness application is RWP-capable. MiniChat and Teams are connected via SoX connectors.
→
Target vision growsA newly procured MiniChat is RWP-native. Teams remains integrated via SoX for the time being, until a natural renewal point is reached there as well.
Important: Not all systems need to reach the same level of maturity at the same time. The architecture supports the coexistence of native RWP capability and integration.
This slide is the direct answer to the big-bang concern. A new business application can be procured as RWP-capable, while others remain connected via a SoX connector for years. What matters is a clean boundary of responsibility: In the integrated Teams case, records are managed within SoX. The landscape is allowed to be heterogeneous, as long as the architecture handles this heterogeneity deliberately and in a controlled way.

Scene 8 · The normal development framework

RecordWeb is not rolled out. It is built in step with every change.

1

Orient

Identify relevant information flows and the applications in which context can be lost today.

2

Integrate

Connect suitable initial cases to RecordWeb via a layer such as SoX.

3

Renew

Take RWP capability into architectural account during further development, replacement, and new procurement.

4

Expand

The network grows step by step through further applications, organizations, and business processes.

Procurement as a lever: Public authorities renew applications through regulated procedures anyway. RecordWeb capability can initially be considered as a quality aspect and later become a binding part of the tendered requirements.

Procurement in stages: starting as an evaluation criterion, later a more heavily weighted award criterion. The decisive point is: The normal lifecycle of applications (further development, EoL, procurement) becomes the transformation mechanism.

Scene 9 · The architecture decision

The question is not: "Which system replaces which?"

The strategic question is:

What shared architecture layer do we need so that records from different applications remain identifiable, linkable, and reachable throughout their entire lifespan?

Without a shared layer

  • New applications create new information boundaries
  • Context is copied, exported, or transferred manually
  • Integration remains isolated and project-based
  • Long-term traceability is established after the fact

With RecordWeb as the target framework

  • New applications become connectable step by step
  • Records remain networked through relationships
  • Integration and native capabilities can coexist
  • Traceability becomes part of the originating architecture
RecordWeb is, first and foremost, an architecture decision: What properties should new and further-developed applications bring with them in the future, and what shared services or profiles do we need to provide for this?

Scene 10 · What belongs on the roadmap

A first architectural step is small, but sets the direction.

1. Confirm the target vision

Recognize RecordWeb as a long-term reference framework for networked, cross-application records.

2. Use the pilot architecture

Use the Question Time PoC with question management, answer management, MiniChat, Teams integration, SoX, conformance checking, and MiniChat migration through to archiving as a learning environment.

3. Align renewals

Specifically review connectability for suitable further developments, replacements, and procurements.

The first decision is not to rebuild everything. It is to no longer plan new information islands without connectability in the future.
This leads to a concrete mandate: formulate architecture principles, develop a reference profile, select another integration case, or include RWP capability in an upcoming procurement. The Question Time PoC is not the final proof for this, but a vivid subject for discussion and learning.
