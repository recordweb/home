RecordWeb · For implementers

RWP for implementers.

How applications can not merely store records, but manage them as linked, accountable, and long-term connectable information objects.

Your application doesn't have to become the only system.
It can become an accountable node in the RecordWeb.
This presentation targets vendors of records management, GEVER (Swiss electronic records management), specialist, and collaboration applications. It is not a complete implementation manual. It explains what RWP means for a product architecture, which capabilities can emerge step by step, and why this is an attractive development path for both customers and vendors.

Scene 1 · The product reality

Your application already matters, but it isn't alone.

In public administration, business-relevant information arises in specialist applications, chat, email, office tools, records management, and portals.

Your customers still expect this information to eventually connect, remain understandable, and be available across system boundaries when needed.

Software providerdevelops a specialist or records-management application

The opening honours the existing product landscape. RWP does not demand that every application master all information or replace a new central system. It offers a shared path so that applications can keep their own relevant records and connect with other applications.

Scene 2 · The integration trap

Today, relationships are often rebuilt per application and per project.

Point-to-point integration

Every new interface connects two products for one specific purpose, with its own semantics and its own operational responsibility.

Retroactive capture

Relevant work information is exported, copied, or manually transferred into another system.

System retirement

At end of life, data, relationships, and evidence are often only cleaned up, converted, and reconstructed years later.

Customers don't just buy a feature. They buy the ability to manage their information responsibly across applications and over time.
This is not a criticism of existing vendors. Today's landscape grew this way historically. The opportunity is that a shared protocol can reduce the cost of repeated one-off integration and later reconstruction.

Scene 3 · The role of RWP

RWP is a shared layer — not a new specialist application.

Your applicationBusiness logic, process, user interface, and your own data stay with you.
→
RWP capabilityRecords get identity, status, versions, relationships, and a resolvable context.
→
RecordWebOther authorised applications can reference, verify, and incorporate records into their own business context.
RWP does not standardise your business process. It creates connectability for the records that arise within your business process.
This is the most important architectural distinction. RWP is not competing with a vendor's domain logic. Nor is it a dossier product. RWP describes a shared information and trust layer for records, so a product can keep its strengths and still become part of a cross-application network.

Scene 4 · The four product capabilities

RWP capability begins with four clear responsibilities.

1

Manage a record

Create, edit, and finalise business-relevant information as a standalone record.

2

Resolve identity

Provide a stable record reference and make it resolvable via your own namespace or resolver.

3

Express relationships

Connect records with cases, sources, results, triggers, or other records.

4

Support transition

At end of life, enable a controlled handover to a successor application or an AIS.

These are deliberately product-oriented capabilities. A first implementation doesn't need to cover every possible relationship or protocol detail. What matters is that the application can responsibly manage its own records, make them resolvable, and not confine its context to a closed product container.

Scene 5 · A record lifecycle

The record becomes final in the work process, not only in a later handover project.

1Create

The application creates a record with a type, accountable identity, and the necessary context.

2Edit

Drafts and working states can continue to be developed as long as the record is not yet finalised.

3Finalise

An authorised person or role marks a binding version and protects it from silent change.

4Provide

The record remains resolvable via its accountable context and endpoint.

5Transfer

At end of life, a controlled custodyTransfer to a successor application or an AIS.

Finalisation is a professional and technical turning point: the record is ready, even while its application keeps running for many more years.
This slide matters for vendors because it connects familiar lifecycle logic to RWP language. Finalisation is not necessarily archiving. As long as the application is operating, it remains responsible for provision. Only end of life triggers a controlled custodyTransfer, either to a successor application or to an AIS.

This simplified DID document comes from the PoC "Question time". For vendors, the separation matters: the DID is the stable reference. The resolver is the resolution mechanism. The endpoint is where the accountable application provides the record. At transition, the endpoint may change while the DID as a reference stays constant.

Scene 7 · Rules an application can fulfil

A schema is not just a data form. It is a versioned contract about which properties a RecordType should have.
The key message: vendors can implement and test clearly which rules a RecordType must satisfy. The SchemaRecord is itself a versioned and revisable artefact. In further governance, ConformanceRecords can be bound to a schema and document that a concrete application and product version were checked against these rules.

Scene 9 · Implementation must be verifiable

Interoperability doesn't come from self-declaration, but from tests.

✓DID resolution

Can an independent client resolve the DID and find the accountable endpoint?

✓Schema binding

Can the application prove which schema and which schema version it uses to create a record?

✓Integrity and finalisation

Are payload, snapshot, and final version traceable — are unauthorised changes prevented?

The question isn't only "does the product support RWP?" It's also: which profiles, roles, and test cases does this specific product version demonstrably satisfy?
This slide references the conformance tests used in the PoC without claiming full certification here. A ConformanceRecord can precisely capture: product, product version, deployment, roles, profile, test tool, evidence, results, and validity period. This gives vendors a traceable basis for quality and customer trust.

Scene 10 · A realistic development path

RWP capability can grow into a product iteratively.

1

Scope

Select one or two RecordTypes with high business value.

2

Manage

Manage these records in the product with identity, status, finalisation, schema, and a traceable version.

3

Connect

Add resolver capability and relationships to cases, sources, or records of other applications.

4

Demonstrate

Build profiles, tests, and, where relevant, conformance evidence for specific product versions.

No big bang: a first RWP capability can start small, but should from the outset produce real, resolvable, and accountable records.

The roadmap is deliberately product-near. A vendor can start with a RecordType that creates clear value for its customers. What matters is not stopping at a pure export function, but building a record lifecycle that can later be extended and networked with other applications.

Scene 11 · What we can define together

Vendor knowledge and RecordWeb governance belong together.

Software vendors know their products, deployment realities, and integration limits. RecordWeb creates an open framework for shared requirements, profiles, test cases, and interoperability.

The next sensible question is therefore not "can you implement everything tomorrow?"

But rather: which RecordType and which concrete use case are suitable for a first, demonstrable RWP integration?
