RecordWeb · A story for archives

When an application ends.

How RecordWeb accompanies records and their context across system changes.

Not rescuing at the last moment, when the system disappears.
Accompanying the transition from the start.
This presentation doesn't show a model where every completed answer is archived immediately. It shows a different moment: an RWP-conformant application reaches its end of life. Then the record must be transferred in a controlled way — without reinventing its content or its identity.

Scene 3 · The real risk

And what if the application disappears?

https://minichat.example.gov/api/records/787475d4-…
404Record not foundThe application has been retired. The old endpoint is no longer available.

When applications end, it's not enough to just rescue files. Without a prepared transition, resolution is lost, provenance becomes unclear, relationships go missing, and costly reconstruction follows.

This slide is deliberately provocative — not because every system replacement inevitably leads to a 404 page, but because exactly this experience makes the risk visible: application gone, link dead, data only available as a hard-to-read export, context unclear. A rescue effort afterwards often begins only under time pressure.

Not gathering for ten years first and then cleaning up, converting, and preparing. The relevant effort shifts to the right point in time: to the creation and finalisation of the record.
This is the central message for an archive: today, dedicated projects often arise around disposal and submission that clean, convert, enrich, and re-link long-past data — similar to the effort of the paper era. RecordWeb doesn't replace every check, but it reduces the structural burden of later reconstruction.

Scene 5 · End of life is the trigger

Only when the application ends is a transfer needed.

It's not the finalisation of the answer that triggers archiving. The trigger is the end of life of the application that has been providing the record.

RWP-conformant applicationThe record stays unchanged and resolvable via the namespace.
→
End of LifeThe application is replaced or decommissioned.
→
custodyTransferThe record moves, in a controlled way, into a new custody context.
The term custodyTransfer is used deliberately here. It's about a regulated handover of the custody and provisioning context, not about rewriting or replacing the record itself.

Scene 6 · Two controlled paths

Where does a record go when its application ends?

Path 1

Successor business application

The new application takes on responsibility for the record and continues to provide it via a new, responsible endpoint.

Path 2

AIS

The record is transferred into the archival information system and continues to be provided there in its archival context.

In both cases, the record stays the record. What changes is its custody and provisioning context.
The Question Time PoC can demonstrate the second path: MiniChat is assumed to reach end of life, and a record is transferred via SIP into a MiniAIS.

Important: The SIP doesn't create a replacement record. It provides a handover frame so that AIS can take over the existing record in a controlled way.

In the Question Time PoC, MiniChat is assumed to be an end-of-life application. That's why the record is embedded in a SIP frame for the handover to AIS.

OAIS terminology fits here: SIP as Submission Information Package. But the story stays precise: the SIP is the frame of the handover. The record isn't transformed, isn't recreated, and isn't treated as a mere data package without identity. In practice, a SIP would contain many records; here, per record, for illustration. AIS is likewise strongly simplified, serving only to illustrate that records can be transferred and remain callable under the same DID.

Scene 9 · Trust arises through verification

A transfer must not only be claimed, but verifiable.

✓Finding independently

A RecordFinder can search for and retrieve the record independently of its original application.

✓Checking via the resolver

DID resolution shows which endpoint currently provides the record.

✓Recognising the version

The current version reference allows the provided version to be verified unambiguously.

In the Question Time PoC, the transfer to AIS can be verified with an independent RecordFinder and via resolver resolution.
The Question Time PoC can demonstrate exactly this process. An independent finder can retrieve the record; the resolver check shows AIS as the providing context. This is the kind of verifiable behaviour that can be further refined later.

Scene 10 · Steering earlier instead of rescuing later

Archives can make requirements for the creation environment verifiable.

Not every single piece of information needs to be manually reconstructed after years. Equally important is whether it's traceable how and with what it was created.

SchemaRecord

Describes the structure and rules of a record type. It's itself a versioned, revisable record.

Conformance
verifiable
ConformanceRecord

Records what was checked against which requirements — e.g. application, product version, and checks performed.

Steering instrument: The archive can set requirements and checks earlier — at applications and their creation rules — rather than only in the submission project.

This slide is an outlook, not a claim that every application automatically takes on archival responsibility. It shows a possible steering logic: SchemaRecords define rules, ConformanceRecords document assessments. Both are themselves records, versioned and revisable. This creates a basis for making requirements binding and verifiable.
