RecordWeb explained

The RecordWeb Reference Architecture

RecordWeb does not reinvent the Internet. It provides a shared architecture for ensuring that institutional information remains understandable, identifiable, and verifiable across systems, organisations, and time.

DE EN

The point

A Record is more than a file

A file, database entry, link, or repository can change, be moved, or cease to exist. A Record must nevertheless remain identifiable, understandable, and verifiable over time.

The central distinction

A Record is not identical to its file, storage location, URL, repository, application, or technical carrier.

Example

An official response may first be drafted in a business application, exported as a PDF, transferred to an archive, and later made available through a different service. The technical carrier changes. The Record can still retain the same identity and a verifiable history.

The RecordWeb Reference Architecture, or RWRA, helps distinguish these different questions. It is not an additional network protocol and does not prescribe a particular software architecture.

It describes which concerns belong together: technical infrastructure, information representation, Record and evidence, context, governance, and the institutional use of information.

The six layers

The RecordWeb Reference Stack

The six layers are a conceptual model. They do not mean that each layer must be implemented by a separate system or organisation. A business application can address several layers at the same time.

6

Use and Institutional Effect

What is the Record used for? For example, a decision, accountability, public access, research, or archival use.

5

Governance and Records Continuum

Who is responsible? Which rules apply to access, retention, appraisal, transfer, disposal, and preservation?

4

Context, Relations and Case Views

In which functional, organisational, legal, and temporal context does a Record exist? How does it relate to other Records?

3

Record and Evidence

What is the persistent, citable, and verifiable Record? Identity, state, versions, provenance, integrity, and evidence are central.

The RecordWeb boundary

Above this boundary, the focus is the Record as an institutional object. Below it, the focus is its representations and technical carriers.

2

Information Package and Representation

How is content structured, exchanged, rendered, and interpreted? This includes content, metadata, schemas, files, JSON, JSON-LD, RDF, and other representations.

1

Carrier and Infrastructure

Where and how is information stored, transmitted, replicated, resolved, and preserved technically? This includes repositories, databases, APIs, resolvers, networks, and storage.

RWP focuses primarily on Layer 3: Record and Evidence. It specifies how a Record can be persistently identified, versioned, finalised, verified, and supported by evidence.

How the layers work together

From infrastructure to institutional effect

1–2

Technology and representation

Infrastructure stores and transmits information. Representations make it readable, exchangeable, and processable by machines.

A URL can indicate where something is available today. A file can contain a representation. Neither alone answers what the persistent Record is.

3

Record and evidence

Here, information becomes a verifiable Record: with persistent identity, a defined version, state, integrity, provenance, and associated responsibility.

This layer makes it possible to establish later not only that something exists, but which particular state of a Record was used, finalised, approved, or transferred.

4

Context and relations

A single Record is rarely sufficient. Its meaning also emerges from functions, processes, people, legal bases, subjects, and relations to other Records.

A case or dossier can therefore be generated as a traceable view over related Records, rather than being the sole and permanent container for all information.

5–6

Responsibility and use

Governance determines which obligations apply to a Record. Institutional use determines the effect that the Record can support.

A technically valid Record does not automatically become public, legally binding, or subject to permanent retention. Those outcomes require rules, responsibility, and a specific institutional purpose.

Two perspectives across all layers

Time and trust

The layers describe different concerns. Two questions accompany every layer: How does information remain continuous over time? And why can a person or organisation trust a Record?

Cross-cutting dimension

Time and continuity

A Record can be created, revised, finalised, transferred, migrated, retained, appraised, disposed of, or preserved in an archive.

Its storage location or representation can change during this process. RecordWeb supports continuity through persistent identity, versions, provenance, and verifiable relations.

Cross-cutting dimension

Trust, assurance, and conformance

Trust does not rest on one technical signal. Relevant factors include responsible agents, authority, signatures, integrity evidence, provenance, applicable rules, and independent review.

RecordWeb deliberately distinguishes validation of a particular Record from assessment of a software implementation and from institutional certification.

Where RecordWeb fits

What RWC and RWP contribute

RWC

The concept

The RecordWeb Concept explains the underlying ideas: what a Record is, how it differs from a file, location, or application, and how context, governance, time, and institutional effect relate to it.

RWP

The protocol

The RecordWeb Protocol specifies primarily the Record and Evidence layer: identity, Record types, states, versions, integrity, provenance, and verification.

Not prescribed

The Reference Architecture does not prescribe a particular repository, database, resolver, API, programming language, or user interface.

Instead, it helps organisations design interfaces and responsibilities so that Records remain understandable and verifiable when systems, formats, or storage locations change.

Publishing conformance records

Evidence can be made discoverable

An organisation may publish a ConformanceRecord, or a verifiable reference to one. This can explain which RecordWeb profiles and roles a particular software version claims, and on which basis that claim is made.

Possible approaches include an organisational website, a procurement or audit evidence repository, an institutional registry, a DID-related service, or a signed Nanopublication. Direct provision to a procuring organisation, auditor, or other verifier can also be appropriate.

Important

Publication makes a claim easier to find. It does not by itself prove that the claim is true, independently assessed, or certified. Each verifier evaluates the attester, assessment method, evidence, and its own applicable trust policy.

In brief

RecordWeb provides orientation

RecordWeb distinguishes technical storage from persistent identity, information representation from evidence, context from technical links, and governance from mere system properties.

A Record does not remain trustworthy because it sits in one particular place. It remains traceable when its identity, state, history, context, and evidence can still be inspected after systems and representations have changed.

The Reference Architecture shows where these concerns sit within the larger picture. RWP supplies interoperable building blocks for the Record and its evidence. Organisations decide how to embed those building blocks in their applications, governance, and long-term responsibility for information.
