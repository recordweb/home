RecordWeb · A story about responsibility

Responsibility has a place.

How networked records work across organisational boundaries, without responsibility disappearing into the network.

Decentralized doesn't mean unclear.
It means responsibility becomes visible where it actually lies.
This presentation complements the story about trust. Trust is verifiable, but who carries responsibility? Especially when records are linked rather than replicated, it must stay visible which organisation, which application, and which role is responsible for which part of the context.

Scene 2 · The understandable reflex

"Let's just copy the answer into our dossier."

This sentence sounds reasonable at first. A copy seems to provide security: what's on our side is under our control.

But every copy raises new questions: which version is authoritative? Who updates it? What happens if responsibility, version, or provisioning change?

This slide doesn't morally devalue copies. It shows their cost. A copy can be necessary for certain purposes, but as a default solution for relationships between organisations it multiplies coordination effort, version risk, and uncertainty about the accountable original.

Scene 3 · Linking with clear boundaries

A relationship connects. Responsibility stays with the respective record.

Parliament

The QuestionRecord stays within parliament's sphere of responsibility.

Parliament is responsible for its identity, status, provisioning, and business context.

↔

Government

The AnswerRecord stays within the government's sphere of responsibility.

The government is responsible for processing, finalisation, version, and provisioning of the answer.

The relationship says: "This answer responds to this question." It doesn't say: "One side takes over responsibility for the other's record."
This is the central rule: linking is not ownership. A record can be connected to a record from another party without that organisation becoming the custodian of the other's record. This is exactly what enables a network of accountable nodes, rather than a collection of unclear copies.

Scene 4 · Responsibility is more than ownership

Several roles can be visible without replacing one another.

E

Creating person

Ms. Roth or a team creates, checks, and processes a record in its subject-matter context.

R

Responsible office

An organisation or application is responsible for the custody, finalisation, and provisioning of its record.

A

Assessing body

The archive or an independent assessment body can evaluate conformance against defined rules.

U

Using person

Parliament, the public, an archive, or a subject-matter expert uses the record within permitted scope.

An assessment doesn't confirm subject-matter responsibility. A link doesn't transfer custodial responsibility. Roles need to be kept apart.
This slide prevents an important confusion. The attesting body isn't automatically the owner or operator. The using organisation isn't automatically responsible for creation. And the person who creates a record isn't necessarily the office that provides it long term. RecordWeb can make these roles visible and keep their relationships traceable.

Scene 5 · The application has responsibility too

The responsible application is not a neutral storage location.

As long as an application is in operation, it manages records within its sphere of responsibility. It must identify its records, maintain their status and version, and provide a reference point — an endpoint.

This slide connects responsibility with application architecture. The application isn't merely a technical location; it takes on a role in maintaining and providing its records. Other organisations can rely on this provisioning when responsibility and conformance are traceable.

Scene 6 · Decentralized, but not arbitrary

A network needs clear boundaries of responsibility.

Without clear boundaries

  • Multiple copies look like multiple originals
  • Unclear who may update a version
  • Unclear who must guarantee availability
  • Assessment bodies, applications, and subject-matter offices blur their roles
  • A system change triggers a responsibility debate

With clear boundaries

  • Every record has an accountable context
  • Relationships allow networking without change of ownership
  • Status and version are assigned to the responsible record
  • Conformance can verify creation and custody rules
  • An EoL triggers a defined transition
The goal isn't to distribute responsibility. The goal is to make it visible and verifiable within a distributed architecture.
This slide summarises the governance logic. Decentralisation must not become an excuse for "nobody is responsible." On the contrary: the architecture must make clear who is responsible for which record and for which handover.

Scene 7 · Responsibility can transfer

When an application ends, responsibility isn't handed over invisibly.

At the end of an application's life, a controlled handover — custodyTransfer — is needed. The record stays the same; its custody and provisioning context changes traceably.

Previous application

Manages the record as long as it's in operation and responsible.

→
custodyTransfer

Documented handover of custodial and provisioning responsibility.

→
Successor application or AIS

Takes on responsibility and provides the existing record in the new context.

This slide directly connects to the archive story. It's not finalisation that triggers a transfer. The handover becomes necessary when the previously responsible application reaches its end of life. custodyTransfer makes clear: this is a regulated transfer of responsibility, not files disappearing and later being rediscovered.

Scene 8 · Responsibility becomes verifiable

Other organisations don't just have to believe in responsibility.

For parliament

It can link to the government's answer and check which office manages it, which version applies, and under which rules it was created.

For the government

It can link to parliament's question without taking over the original, and check the referenced context.

For the archive

It can set requirements for custody, conformance, and controlled handover, instead of only rescuing holdings afterwards.

Interplay: Clear responsibility makes linking possible. Verifiability makes that trust resilient. A regulated handover secures responsibility when an application ends.

Three themes come together here: responsibility, trust, and continuity. This presentation doesn't claim all conflicts are automatically solved, but shows an architecture in which the decisive questions become visible and governable.

RecordWeb · Responsibility architecture

A network of records doesn't need central ownership.

It needs self-contained, accountable records, explicit relationships, verifiable rules, and a traceable transition when responsibility changes.

Not everything belongs to everyone.
But everything stays accountable within its context.
